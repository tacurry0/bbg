# V23: shared live play and swipe running

All new fair fixture plays, including CPU offense, use the same physical runner/possession/throw state. A fielder must pick up/catch, reach a receiver's bag and deliver the throw; outs are evaluated against the runner's touched bases. The update substep is split at throw arrivals, and equal arrivals favor the runner. Force chains disappear when trailing runners are retired. Third force/batter outs cancel all runs; later third tag outs retain earlier valid crossings. Home runs and the previously requested fixed-probability bunt outcomes remain awarded plays.

Manual running replaces the button panel with a larger interactive diamond. Swipe an individual runner toward the next/previous adjacent base; a single gesture never queues another leg. The batter initially runs to first. Other runners wait for commands, including forced runners; failing to send a forced runner can lead to a force out. Every arrival stops and clears the instruction. Traffic blocks the moving runner instead of pushing a waiting runner backward or permitting a pass. Fly catches require retouching; user-controlled runners receive no automatic return command. After the defense has possession and all runners are safely stationary, the play closes after a five-second decision window (scaled with field speed); new gestures reset it. A stopped runner remains on that base into the next at-bat. Safe base occupancy and minimap marker coordinates derive from the same runner progress as the field model.

CPU pitch choice now uses count, repertoire, repetition, batter strength and verified pitch usage when available. Strike-zone edges, two-strike chase pitches and three-ball strike preference are shared by opposing pitchers and automatic defense. CPU hitting uses count, measured walk/strikeout tendencies and contact/movement/velocity to vary takes, chases and timing.

Flight interception uses reachable trajectory samples, rather than always running to a landing point. Grounders, liners, bloopers and wall contacts are identified; a ball caught after hitting the wall is never an air out. Auto runners evaluate live retrieval/throw time and can attempt extra bases without prescribing a hit/out outcome in advance.

Postgame reports contain inning scores, both teams' batting and pitching tables, highlighted contributors and game-standard pitcher decisions. Runs retain their original responsible pitcher across relief and pinch running. Winning pitcher is the pitcher in the batting team's current slot when it takes the final lead; this shortened-game rule does not reproduce official minimum-inning/discretionary-reliever scoring. Loser is the pitcher responsible for that run. Reports are retained with pennant results.

## Player evidence

`data/player-matchup-evidence-2026.json` contains primary published sources. `python scripts/build-matchups.py` joins them to existing CPBL/APBC identities without rerating the baseline abilities. 29 Japanese/Korean hitters have verified opposing-pitcher-type split totals, with all AB/H/HR/K/BB season totals reconciled. KBO R merges official R+U and the UI says right/submarine, not a pure right-handed split. Published split PA is unavailable; AB is used as a conservative shrinkage sample. Only measured AVG/SLG fields alter contact/power, with 120 AB shrinkage; unknown SLG/PA remain null. Taiwan split data was unavailable and is not fabricated. 123 position evidence entries and existing Japanese pitch evidence are shown; detailed field/arm and break movement remain estimates. OF alone never asserts actual experience at all three outfield positions.
